अभिलेख · Abhilekh

Your medical history follows you, not a plastic bag.

तपाईंको स्वास्थ्यको इतिहास प्लास्टिकको झोलामा होइन, तपाईंसँगै हिँड्छ।

A planned health record for Nepal that the patient owns. You photograph your prescriptions and lab reports once, and any doctor you choose can see them on a phone, for as long as you allow.

How it is meant to work यसरी चल्ने योजना छ

  1. 1. Photographफोटो खिच्नुहोस्

    Take a photo of a prescription or lab report with your phone. The photo itself is kept as the record.

  2. 2. Readपढ्छ

    Two separate readers read the page. A value is filled in only when both agree; anything unclear is left empty.

  3. 3. Confirmतपाईं जाँच्नुहुन्छ

    You check each value against a crop of your own paper. Nothing enters your record until you confirm it.

  4. 4. Shareडाक्टरलाई देखाउनुहोस्

    Show a QR code to a doctor. They see your history on their phone, with no account, for 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days.

Icons are illustrations.

What Abhilekh will and will not do के गर्छ, के गर्दैन

It gives no advice.

It shows what the paper says: the value, the unit, the range the lab printed and any flag the lab printed. It does not explain results, add warnings or suggest treatment. Your doctor does that.

कुनै सल्लाह दिँदैन। कागजमा जे लेखिएको छ, त्यही देखाउँछ।

You control who sees it.

Every share has an end time (never "forever"). You see each time it is opened, and you can stop it at once.

कसले, कहिलेसम्म हेर्ने भन्ने तपाईं आफैं तय गर्नुहुन्छ।

Your paper stays the record.

Every value links back to the part of the photo it came from, so a doctor can always check the original.

हरेक अंक तपाईंको कागजको फोटोसँग जोडिएको हुन्छ।

No guessing.

If a value or a medicine name is unclear, it is left for you to check, not guessed. A medicine name that is not on the Department of Drug Administration list is shown as written and marked.

Simple to sign in.

Your mobile number and one SMS code the first time, then a 6-digit PIN on your phone.

Where things stand अहिलेको अवस्था

  • Abhilekh is at the planning stage, built as a Nepali social enterprise and free for patients.
  • A prototype is being tested on invented sample papers only. No real patient's papers are used.
  • The plan is a small first pilot in Kathmandu. Who takes part, and when, has not been decided.
