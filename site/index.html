A patient-controlled health record for Nepal

Your medical history follows you, not a plastic bag.

तपाईंको स्वास्थ्यको इतिहास प्लास्टिकको झोलामा होइन, तपाईंसँगै हिँड्छ।

Animation · illustration, not a real record

Every new doctor asks for the old reports. They live in a plastic bag, and pages go missing. Abhilekh is being planned to end that.

नयाँ डाक्टरले पुराना रिपोर्ट माग्छन्। ती प्लास्टिकको झोलामा हुन्छन्, अनि पाना हराउँछन्।

How it is meant to work · यसरी चल्ने योजना छ

01

Photograph फोटो खिच्नुहोस्

Take a photo of a prescription or lab report with your phone. The photo itself is kept as the record, never overwritten.

02

Read पढ्छ

Two separate readers read the page. A value is filled in only when both agree. Anything unclear is left empty for you, never guessed.

03

Confirm तपाईं जाँच्नुहुन्छ

You check each value against a crop of your own paper. Nothing enters your record until you confirm it.

04

Share डाक्टरलाई देखाउनुहोस्

Show a QR code to a doctor. They see your history on their phone, with no account, for 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days. You can stop it at any time.

What it will and will not do के गर्छ, के गर्दैन

No advice

It shows what the paper says: value, unit, the range and any flag the lab printed. It does not explain results, add warnings or suggest treatment. Your doctor does that.

कुनै सल्लाह दिँदैन। कागजमा जे छ, त्यही देखाउँछ।

You control who sees it

Every share has an end time, never "forever". You see each time it is opened, and you can stop it at once.

कसले, कहिलेसम्म हेर्ने, तपाईं आफैं तय गर्नुहुन्छ।

Your paper stays the record

Every value links back to the part of the photo it came from, so a doctor can always check the original.

हरेक अंक तपाईंको कागजको फोटोसँग जोडिएको हुन्छ।

No guessing

An unclear value or medicine name is left for you to check. A name not on the Department of Drug Administration list is shown as written and marked.

Simple to sign in

Your mobile number and one SMS code the first time, then a 6-digit PIN on your phone.

Where things stand अहिलेको अवस्था

  1. Abhilekh is at the planning stage, planned as a Nepali social enterprise, free for patients.
  2. A prototype is being tested on invented sample papers only. No real patient's papers are used.
  3. The plan is a small first pilot in Kathmandu. Who takes part, and when, has not been decided.
